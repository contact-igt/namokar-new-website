# Design notes

19 Oct 2026. Three live references were opened in the in-app browser (narrow pane, so mobile-style layouts) to read how established eye hospitals lead and organise their pages. Principles only; nothing was copied. The earlier Nuvica, Synora and Pixel Eye reference screenshots from the first session are not available in this session, so they were not re-inspected.

| Reference (seen 19 Oct 2026) | What it showed |
|---|---|
| [Moorfields Eye Hospital](https://www.moorfields.nhs.uk/) | The hospital building photograph fills the hero beside one calm solid panel carrying a short headline; a quiet "For patients" section follows |
| [Aravind Eye Care System](https://www.aravind.org/) | A stack of full-width banner images (an admissions notice, then the hospitals) with small text links under them |
| [Wills Eye Hospital](https://www.willseye.org/) | A full-bleed photograph of clinicians with a flat red headline card; an alert bar above; the headline is a superlative claim |

## Five takeaways applied

1. **Lead with the real place and real people.** A genuine photograph of the institution in the first screen builds more trust than illustration (Moorfields). Namokar's hero and treatment previews use its own rooms and equipment.
2. **One calm text panel per photograph.** A solid or near-solid area behind the headline keeps it readable over imagery. The treatment preview puts its title, summary and link on a near-white card over the photograph.
3. **Put "for patients" navigation immediately below the hero**, in plain words. The "Find care" explorer is the first section after the banner.
4. **One idea per screen, generous spacing, few colours.** Moorfields and Wills use a single brand colour; Namokar keeps orange to rules, selected states and links.
5. **Show the next step, not decoration.** Each treatment names its action ("Explore treatment") and the call and directions stay visible.

## Five anti-patterns avoided

1. **Banner stacks and announcement bars** competing for the first fold (Aravind, Wills): no alert strip, one banner.
2. **Superlative headlines** ("the top ophthalmologists in America"): breaks the claims rule and invites distrust. None added.
3. **Small text links under big images** as the main navigation: treatments are large, labelled, tappable.
4. **Hover-only discovery:** every treatment is a visible link or card on every device.
5. **Decoration without function:** no floating blobs, glass panels, particle effects, repeated icon cards or aurora gradients. Gradients are three quiet washes of warm white and graphite.
