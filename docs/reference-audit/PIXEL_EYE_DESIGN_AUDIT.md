# Pixel Eye reference audit (19 Oct 2026)

## Access and method

- **Live site** `https://pixel-eye-web-frontend-ebon.vercel.app` loaded in the built-in browser and in headless Chromium at 390x844 and 1440x900. Screenshots of the home page (first screen and full page), `/service`, `/service/cataract`, `/service/glaucoma`, `/doctors` and `/about` at both widths are in `screenshots/pixel-eye/` (git-ignored: they are another organisation's pages). Behaviour was exercised on the home page only: hero auto-advance, care-area links, keyboard Tab order. **Hover and touch behaviour of the inner pages were not tested.**
- **Source** `https://github.com/contact-igt/pixel-eye-2.git`: `gh repo view` reports it public, default branch `main`, **no licence** (so nothing may be copied; all rights stay with the owner). Cloned read-only and shallow into the scratchpad outside the Namokar project (1 GB, mostly media), nothing run, nothing installed, nothing pushed. Files actually read: `package.json`, `src/component/Home/Banner/index.jsx`, `src/component/Home/Care/index.jsx`, `src/common/FloatingCta/index.jsx`, `src/constant/heroBannerContent.js`, `src/constant/homeContent.js` (slides). Other components were looked at only through the rendered pages. No Pixel Eye image, text, brand mark, claim or component was copied.
- The brief also names `https://www.pixeleyehospitals.com/`; it is not the Vercel reference and was not inspected.

## What the reference actually is

| Observed pattern and evidence | Appropriate Namokar adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Hero:** one large rounded-corner photograph (a child leaping between two adults, clearly lifestyle or stock imagery) with the headline and a Book Appointment pill on top; the logo and the NABH badge sit in notches cut into the frame. Source: a two-slide banner that moves on a `setInterval` of 8.5 s; the image track is `aria-hidden`. Live test: the headline changed by itself within 10 s ("When diabetes blurs your world"); the hero has **no previous, next or pause control**. | **Rejected:** auto-advance with no pause and a photograph that is not the hospital. **Adopted in spirit:** one large, calm, image-led hero frame with the message overlaid beside it. Namokar keeps its manual four-slide carousel (arrows, tabs, swipe, arrow keys, no autoplay) and puts real facility and equipment photographs in it. |
| **Navigation:** floating pill bar in the page's top margin with Home, About Us, Doctors, Treatment (dropdown), Appointment, Blog, a search icon; the logo in a notch. Live test: Tab order starts with the hero's "Book Appointment" buttons, **before** the navigation; there is no skip link; the focus style is the browser default 1 px; 6 of 103 images have no alt text. | Namokar's header already has a skip link, a services menu and a Call button; nothing to copy. Noted as things not to repeat. |
| **Always-available actions:** a vertical rail on desktop (WhatsApp, YouTube, phone) and, on phones, a fixed bottom bar (Phone, WhatsApp, Instagram, Book, Address). Source: `FloatingCta` (lucide icons, tel and wa.me links). | **Adopted (rebuilt, not copied):** a phone-only bottom bar with the two actions that matter and that Namokar may show: the appointment action (it follows `BookButton`: "Call to Book an Appointment" until online requests exist) and Directions. No WhatsApp, Instagram or hours: not approved for Namokar. |
| **"Featured Care Areas":** three tall numbered cards (01 to 03) that overlap; one is "active" and larger. Source: `Care/index.jsx` keeps an `activeId` (default centre), resets it `onMouseLeave`, and on mobile an `IntersectionObserver` activates whichever card crosses the middle of the viewport. Cards are links ("Learn about ..."). Only three of eight services are featured; the rest are behind "View All Treatments". | **Adopted in principle:** numbered, image-led treatment entries and an "active" state that follows the pointer or focus. Namokar's explorer already does this for all six services, with the preview always visible, hover intent, focus and first-tap navigation. Not adopted: scroll-triggered activation on phones (it changes content under the reader's thumb). |
| **Doctors:** a single "Our Founders" band with two cut-out portraits on a teal panel, name and qualifications beside each; the doctors are not repeated elsewhere on the home page. | **Adopted:** the doctor appears once on the home page, in one introduction. (Namokar's own page was repeating her photograph about 7 times; see the image matrix.) |
| **Photography:** stock or generic lifestyle photographs and a split before/after-style eye image; patient "stories" use a mosaic of stock-looking faces with star ratings. | **Rejected:** stock faces, star ratings, before/after styling. Namokar uses only photographs of the centre and original line drawings. |
| **Numbers and credentials:** NABH badge, "1,200+", "25,000+", "99% cataract success", an Insurance and Cashless section. | **Rejected:** none of this may appear for Namokar without the client's documents (project rule). |
| **Palette and type:** blue and periwinkle gradients, a rounded geometric sans, large pill buttons. | **Rejected:** Namokar stays orange and graphite with Manrope and Inter. Warm, restrained gradients only. |
| **Stack:** Pages Router, Next 16.2.6, React 19.2.4, react-slick, GSAP, framer-motion, Bootstrap, vitest; page content in `src/constant/*.js`. | Nothing imported. Namokar is App Router, CSS-first motion, no carousel library. |
| **Length:** the home page is about 9,400 px tall at 1440 (about 9,100 at 390): eleven sections. | Namokar's home now has a clear order (below) and drops its duplicate services section. |

## Principles carried into Namokar

1. One large, image-led hero; real place first. 2. The doctor once, in the right place. 3. Numbered, image-led treatment entry points where each one has its own picture. 4. Thumb-reach actions on phones. 5. Clear separation of sections by background rather than decoration.

## Principles refused

Auto-advancing hero without controls; stock people; ratings, statistics, accreditation and insurance claims; scroll-activated content; a hero that is not the hospital.
