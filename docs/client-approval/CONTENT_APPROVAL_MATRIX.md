### 3.1 Cataract Surgery

- **Page address:** `/stitchless-cataract-surgery`
- **Browser title:** Cataract Surgery | Namokar Eye & Oculoplasty Centre
- **Search description:** General information about cataracts and cataract surgery, and how to arrange an eye examination at Namokar Eye & Oculoplasty Centre in Ashok Vihar, New Delhi.
- **Status:** **Awaiting medical review** — general medical information written for this site; no clinician has reviewed it

> **Headline:** Cataract surgery, explained clearly.  
> **Label above headline:** Stitchless cataract surgery  
> **Lead:** Understand what a cataract is, when surgery is considered and what the first consultation involves.  
> **What is a cataract?**  
> A cataract is a clouding of the eye's natural lens. It usually develops slowly, and it is one of the common reasons that vision becomes blurred or dim as people get older.  
> Cataract surgery removes the clouded lens and, in most cases, replaces it with a clear artificial lens, called an intraocular lens (IOL). Not everyone with a cataract needs surgery straight away. The decision depends on how much your vision affects your daily life and on what the examination shows.  
> **When to have your eyes checked**  
> • Vision that looks cloudy, blurred or dim  
> • Glare from bright light or oncoming headlights, especially at night  
> • Colours that look faded or yellowed  
> • Spectacles that need changing again and again  
> • Reading, driving or recognising faces becoming harder  
> Sudden loss of vision, a new eye injury, or severe eye pain needs urgent medical attention. Do not wait for a routine appointment.  
> — Choosing a lens / About replacement lenses / Artificial lenses come in different designs. Some are made mainly for distance vision, some also help with near vision, and some are designed to correct astigmatism. / Which design, if any, suits you depends on your eyes, your daily activities and your expectations. The doctor explains the choices and what each can and cannot do. No lens can promise that you will never need spectacles.  
> **What an assessment involves** — A general outline. The doctor decides which steps you need and explains each one.  
> 1. Examination: Your vision and the health of your eyes are checked, including the lens.  
> 2. Discussion: The doctor explains whether surgery is likely to help, what the options are and what the risks are.  
> 3. Planning: If you decide to go ahead, measurements of the eye are used to help choose the replacement lens.  
> 4. Surgery, if you choose it: The centre explains how the operation is arranged and what to do before and afterwards.  
> 5. Follow-up: The doctor advises on eye drops and check-ups after the operation.  
> **Questions patients often ask**  
> Q: Do cataracts need surgery straight away?  
> A: Not always. If a cataract is not troubling you, the doctor may suggest checking it from time to time. Surgery is considered when it affects what you need to do in daily life.  
> Q: What is an intraocular lens (IOL)?  
> A: It is the clear artificial lens placed in the eye to replace the natural lens that has become cloudy. Different designs exist, and the doctor discusses which may suit you.  
> Q: What does “stitchless” mean?  
> A: Modern cataract surgery is often done through a very small opening that may not need stitches. Whether stitches are needed depends on the eye and on the technique the surgeon chooses, and this is explained to you.  
> Q: Is cataract surgery suitable for everyone?  
> A: Suitability is decided after an examination. Other eye conditions and general health can affect the plan, and every operation carries some risk, which the doctor goes through with you.  
> Q: What should I bring to my first visit?  
> A: Previous prescriptions and eye reports, your current spectacles and a list of the medicines you take.

### 3.2 Glaucoma Management

- **Page address:** `/glaucoma-management`
- **Browser title:** Glaucoma Management | Namokar Eye & Oculoplasty Centre
- **Search description:** General information about glaucoma, why regular eye examinations matter, and how to arrange an assessment at Namokar Eye & Oculoplasty Centre in Ashok Vihar, New Delhi.
- **Status:** **Awaiting medical review** — general medical information written for this site; no clinician has reviewed it

> **Headline:** Glaucoma care starts with regular check-ups.  
> **Lead:** Glaucoma often has no early symptoms. Examination and long-term follow-up are how it is found and kept under watch.  
> **What is glaucoma?**  
> Glaucoma is a group of eye conditions in which the optic nerve, which carries what the eye sees to the brain, is gradually damaged. Raised pressure inside the eye is a common factor, but it is not the only one.  
> Glaucoma is usually a long-term condition that is managed rather than cured. Damage to the optic nerve cannot be reversed, so the aim of care is to find glaucoma early and to protect the vision that remains. Treatment and follow-up are planned for each person.  
> **Who should have regular eye checks**  
> • Anyone with a close relative who has glaucoma  
> • People who are getting older, because the risk rises with age  
> • People who are very short-sighted  
> • People who use steroid medicines over a long period, as advised by their doctor  
> • People who have had an injury to the eye  
> Sudden eye pain with redness, halos around lights, headache or sickness needs urgent medical attention.  
> — Why check-ups matter / Often no early symptoms. That is why an examination matters. / Vision lost to glaucoma does not come back, and early on most people notice nothing. Regular examination is how it is found, and how any change is caught.  
> — Monitoring / What monitoring may include / Which tests are needed, and how often, is for your doctor to decide. / Eye pressure / The pressure inside the eye is measured, because raised pressure is a common factor in glaucoma. / The optic nerve / The nerve at the back of the eye is examined for the changes that glaucoma causes. / Side vision / A test of the field of vision shows whether any part of it has been lost, often without the person noticing.  
> **What an assessment involves** — A general outline of long-term care. The doctor decides which tests you need and how often.  
> 1. Examination: Your eyes are examined, including the optic nerve at the back of the eye.  
> 2. Tests chosen by the doctor: Tests may measure the pressure in the eye and the field of vision. Which tests are needed is the doctor's decision.
